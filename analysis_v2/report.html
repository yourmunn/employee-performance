01

Eight groups, ordered by priority

A person's group is three comparisons: is their exhaustion above the median, their stress above the median, their deep work above the median? No model is needed to place someone.

The second row is the one that does not show up anywhere else. High exhaustion, high stress, and high deep work: burnout 67, productivity 76. They are still delivering, so no performance review flags them.

02

What moves each outcome

Each habit's effect, holding all the others constant. At 4.6 million rows almost every coefficient is "statistically significant", so a second bar is applied: the effect also has to be large enough to matter.

03

Deep work is not what exhausts people

Output climbs steeply with hours of deep work. Burnout stays flat across the band where 86% of employees sit.

Managers do not face a trade-off here. Protecting people from burnout and letting them concentrate are separate levers, and pulling the second one costs nothing on the first.

04

What each change is associated with

Differences between real groups that match on the other two habits — not projections from a regression line.

05

How the number of groups was decided

Re-running the clustering with different random seeds is the usual reliability test. It is not sufficient, and relying on it produced the wrong answer first time.

A number of groups can be perfectly reproducible on one fixed dataset and still fall apart when the employees change — on a fixed dataset the coin always lands the same way. The structure has eight cells because three habits split two ways each; forcing seven makes the algorithm merge one cell, and which cell it merges depends on the sample.

06

What this cannot support

Not cause

Everything here is association. Nothing shows that lowering someone's stress would lower their burnout.

Not targeted lifestyle advice

Groups do not differ on sleep, screen time, exercise or scrolling — the largest gap between any two groups on any of those is 0.006 of a standard deviation. Advice there is company-wide, not per group.

Not a real workforce

Synthetic data, with inputs independent by construction.

Practical limits

2% of raw rows are missing deep-work hours and cannot be placed. Groups run 10%–15% in size, because medians of discrete 1–10 scales do not split a population evenly.
